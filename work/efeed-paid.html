eFeed · India · 2022–23 · Marketing Lead

A hundred thousand a month, at ten times the return

eFeed is a Cambridge-based biotechnology startup selling into Indian agriculture. I led marketing there for eight months, running the whole paid budget and the lifecycle programme behind it.

Written from the lines of my CV The numbers here are the ones on the CV, and nothing has been added to them

10xReturn on ad spend, up from where the account started
$100kMonthly paid budget, run end to end
$1.5MAnnual revenue from two new product categories

The situation

eFeed sells nutrition and health products to farmers, through retailers and distributors, across India. A $12M business selling a considered purchase to a customer who is rarely online in the way a performance channel assumes.

The paid account had budget and reach. What it did not have was a clear view of which spend produced an order and which spend produced a click that a distributor would have made anyway.

What I changed

  • One budget, one owner. I took the whole $100,000 a month across Google, App campaigns, YouTube and Facebook rather than splitting it by channel team, so the money could move to whichever channel was actually converting that week.
  • Category expansion as a growth lever, not a launch. We opened two new product segments inside the same account structure, which let existing audience and creative learning carry across instead of starting cold.
  • Lifecycle behind the ads. Email, WhatsApp and SMS were built as one programme with A/B tests on segmentation and targeting, so a first order had somewhere to go. I led a team of 15 on this.
  • Feedback from the trade, not just the dashboard. We built market feedback loops with retailers, distributors and farmers, and used what came back to change product communication and positioning rather than only the bidding.

What moved

Return on ad spend reached 10x, with customer acquisition cost falling as the account concentrated on what worked. The two new categories delivered $1.5M in annual revenue.

The lifecycle work carried its own result. Return orders came down from 40% to 15%, which on a physical product is margin rather than vanity, and retention targets were met.

What I would do differently

A 10x return on ad spend is a ratio, and ratios improve when you cut the weakest spend as well as when you improve the best. I would want a geographic holdout running alongside the account from the start, so the share of those orders that would have arrived anyway is measured rather than argued about. I wrote about why that matters in an essay on paid search and incrementality.
