Abicart · Sweden · 2019–22 · Brand Manager

Three million in new annual revenue in the Nordics

Abicart is Sweden's largest eCommerce service provider, a $40M platform business selling to merchants. I ran digital marketing and customer retention across the Nordics for three years.

The situation

A platform sells to merchants who are already looking for a platform. That makes search the centre of the business and makes every other channel an argument about whether it created demand or intercepted it.

The market is four countries with four languages and one buying behaviour, and the sales pipeline was being fed on volume rather than on quality.

What I changed

  • Demand capture before demand creation. The SEO programme came first, because a merchant searching for a store platform is the cheapest customer in the business. Paid was then built around the gaps rather than on top of the same intent.
  • Full funnel across four channels. Google, Facebook, LinkedIn and TikTok were run as one funnel with continuous A/B testing on the target audience, not as four separate budgets each reporting its own success.
  • Pipeline quality as the metric. I optimised the outreach for what the sales team could actually close, working across functions rather than handing over leads and counting them.
  • Positioning written for the product, not the campaign. I developed the go-to-market narratives for core platform features, including store setup, order flows and growth tools, so the ads and the product said the same thing.

What moved

Sign-ups rose 400% and the conversion rate rose 50%, and the two together produced a net $3M in added annual revenue.

The conversion rate matters more than the sign-up figure. Four times the sign-ups at the same conversion rate would have been four times the sales load for the same close rate. Moving both is what made the revenue net rather than gross.

What I would do differently

Three years is long enough to see the ceiling on search. I would start the brand and category work earlier, because by the end we were competing for a fixed pool of merchants already looking, and growth after that comes from merchants who are not looking yet.
