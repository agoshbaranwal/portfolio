Personal project

Witness

A check-in tool I designed and built to get the thing I keep putting off onto a real day, at a real time, and to show me honestly whether I am doing it

Private, built for myself Four surfaces Version 4.18 Designed, written and shipped solo

Why it exists

Every habit app I tried scored me, and a bad week wiped the number back to zero, which is exactly the week you are most likely to quit

So I built the opposite: a tool that asks ten seconds of you each evening, never grades you, and treats a week you missed as pace lost rather than progress erased

It is not open to sign-ups, so this page is the tour

The count only goes up

A week under your own floor reads as behind the pace, with the pace stated, never as a broken streak and never as a zero

A dead phone is not a failure

Days recorded are counted against the days a reminder actually reached you, so a day your phone was off is not held against you

Nothing is scored

There is no grade, no rating and no rank, because the moment a number judges you, you start managing the number

Nobody else sees any of it

No sharing, no feed and no leaderboard, and every answer lives in a Google Sheet in your own account rather than on a server of mine

The week it runs on

The whole product is one loop, and everything else is a detail of these six steps

Sunday

Decide what got done, what you avoided, and the one thing you will do before Wednesday

Sunday

Pin it to a moment and a place, so it is a time or an event in your day rather than sometime this week

That moment

It rings on your phone and in your calendar, at that minute

Wednesday

The check asks whether it happened, and if it did not, it asks for a fifteen-minute version to do tonight

Every evening

Ten seconds to close the day, so tomorrow starts with a plan instead of a debate

Sunday

See it, the week you actually had, and whether your goals are moving

Four rooms, and no others

Everything in the product sits in one of these four, behind a tab bar on a phone and a rail on a desktop, so wherever you are all four are one press away

Today

The day in three parts, your habits, three things you want to get done, and the evening check

Analytics

Every day since day one and what it adds up to: the record, the charts and every entry

Plan

What you are working toward and the habits that get you there: areas, goals, trackers and lists

Settings

Reminders, appearance, this device, how reminders reach you, and the way out

On a phone

The four rooms above, plus the two check-ins that run the week

In a desktop browser

The same single file, laid out again for a wide screen, where the tab bar becomes a rail and the day opens out into two columns

On the Mac

A menu-bar app, not a second website

Most logging happens while you are already at your desk, so the fastest version of it should not need a browser tab

The panel is a small Swift app that logs a habit, shows the week and opens the review, with a keyboard shortcut for each row

It follows the Mac's own light and dark setting, and it carries the same words as every other surface, because two surfaces disagreeing about what the product is would be a defect rather than a backlog item

The reminders

The fourth surface, and the one most products treat as an afterthought

Nothing has to be open for a reminder to arrive, because they are sent by Google's servers on times you set

Each one carries the state of the thing it is about, so the notification answers the question before you open anything, and the two buttons let you log the day without unlocking further

You choose the times, cap how many arrive in a day, hold them until you are at your Mac, and pause everything until a date you pick

How it is put together

Your dataA Google Sheet in your own account, readable in plain rows without Witness
The web appOne file, served by Google Apps Script, laid out for phone and desktop
The Mac panelA native Swift menu-bar app with its own UI
RemindersCalendar alarms, email and web push, sent server-side so nothing needs to be open
