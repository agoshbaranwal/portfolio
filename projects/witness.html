A check-in tool I designed and built to get the thing I keep putting off onto a real day, at a real time, and to show me honestly whether I am doing it

Your dataA Google Sheet in your own account, readable in plain rows without Witness

Built byMe, on my own time: the idea, the design, the code and the writing

The weekly loop

The whole product is one loop that starts on Sunday, checks in on Wednesday, and asks ten seconds of you every evening

The four rooms

Everything in the product sits in one of these four, behind a tab bar on a phone and a rail on a desktop

Four design decisions

Every habit app I tried scored me, and a bad week wiped the number back to zero, which is the week you are most likely to quit

The count only goes up

A week under your own floor reads as behind the pace, with the pace stated, never as a broken streak and never as a zero, and the measure says out loud how it weighs an old day against today

A dead phone is not a failure

Days recorded are counted against the days a reminder actually reached you, so a day your phone was off does not sit in your record as a day you gave up

A day with nothing on it is not a hole

A day you answered with nothing and a day nobody answered are two different marks in your history, because only one of them means you stopped

Nothing is scored

There is no grade, no rating and no rank, only the arithmetic of what you said you would do against the days you have left, so behind and ahead are facts rather than feelings

On a computer

The same product laid out for a wide browser window, and a menu-bar app for logging while you are already at your desk

The Mac menu barA small Swift app that logs a habit, shows the week and opens the review, with a keyboard shortcut on every row, following the Mac's own light and dark setting and carrying the same words as every other surface

The reminders

A reminder carries the state of the thing it is about

Each one carries the state of the thing it is about, so the notification already tells you where you stand, and the two buttons let you log the day without unlocking further

Nothing has to be open for one to arrive, because they are sent by Google's servers at times you set

You choose those times, cap how many arrive in a day, hold them until you are at your Mac, and pause everything until a date you pick
